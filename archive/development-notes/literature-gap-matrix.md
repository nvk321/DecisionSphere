# DecisionSphere — Literature Review & Research Gap Matrix

## 1. Executive Summary

This document conducts a rigorous comparative analysis of existing academic literature and industrial decision-support systems adjacent to **DecisionSphere**. It evaluates prior work across 14 benchmark dimensions to identify what is established versus what constitutes a genuine, experimentally defensible research contribution.

### Grounding Rule for Novelty Claims
DecisionSphere **does not** claim novelty for inventing machine learning algorithms (XGBoost, Random Forests), interpretability methods (SHAP), or solving isolated domain tasks (demand forecasting, late delivery classification, attrition prediction). The potential contribution lies in the **scenario-driven cross-functional decision intelligence framework**: a formal architecture that couples predictive machine learning, explicit parametric scenario propagation, explainable AI, deterministic business-impact accounting, and parametric sensitivity analysis across demand, supply chain, and workforce domains.

## 3. Systematic Identification of Research Gaps

### Gap 1: Siloed Predictive Models vs. Cross-Functional Coupling
*Current State:* The vast majority of ML implementations in operations research focus on a single departmental objective: demand forecasting (Karim et al. 2025), late delivery risk (DataCo benchmarks), or employee attrition (Madanchian & Taherdoost 2025).  
*The Gap:* Strategic decisions (such as launching an aggressive promotional campaign) inherently cross departmental boundaries. Prior systems provide no automated, empirical mechanism to simulate how a sales surge propagates downstream into logistics fulfillment delays and warehouse workforce overtime.  
*DecisionSphere Contribution:* Formalizes an integrated cross-functional propagation engine where an operational lever shifts demand forecasts, scales fulfillment volume, re-evaluates logistics delay risk, and projects workforce overtime strain.

### Gap 2: Monolithic Black-Box / Generative AI vs. Auditable Epistemic Decoupling
*Current State:* Recent enterprise literature attempts to bridge silos using either end-to-end black-box deep learning or generative Large Language Models (Kolb 2024).  
*The Gap:* End-to-end neural networks cannot explain intermediate domain trade-offs, while generative LLMs frequently hallucinate financial figures and risk percentages, violating auditability requirements. Conversely, classic ERP simulation suites (Ivanov 2026) are prohibitively complex and lack lightweight statistical ML integration.  
*DecisionSphere Contribution:* Proposes a strict 6-tier epistemic architecture (`OBSERVED`, `PREDICTED`, `ASSUMED`, `PROPAGATED`, `CALCULATED`, `RECOMMENDED`). Statistical ML is used strictly where data exists (predictions), while financial margins and penalties are computed via deterministic, auditable mathematical accounting.

### Gap 3: Static, Isolated XAI vs. Decision-Oriented Trade-Off Explanation
*Current State:* XAI methods (SHAP, LIME) are widely deployed to explain isolated model predictions (e.g., explaining why an employee has high attrition risk or why a store has high sales).  
*The Gap:* Existing XAI stops at feature attribution plots. It does not connect attributions across domains or explain how a positive driver in one function (e.g., `Promo = 1` driving sales) acts as a risk trigger in another function (e.g., driving logistics delays and overtime).  
*DecisionSphere Contribution:* Links local TreeSHAP attributions directly to scenario levers and recommendation rules, providing an auditable rationale for why specific strategic mitigations (e.g., temporary staffing vs. freight expediting) are recommended.

### Gap 4: Unacknowledged Cross-Domain Assumptions vs. Parametric Sensitivity Analysis
*Current State:* When research studies combine independent data sources or domain equations, they often bury operational assumptions inside code constants without sensitivity testing.  
*The Gap:* Public datasets (Rossmann, DataCo, IBM HR) are heterogeneous and cannot be claimed to represent a single firm. Prior literature rarely investigates how robust strategic decisions are to the coupling parameters that connect these domains.  
*DecisionSphere Contribution:* Formalizes an explicit registry of coupling assumptions (`ASM-001` through `ASM-008`) and incorporates global/local sensitivity analysis (Experiment D) to determine recommendation stability boundaries and parameter tipping points.

## 4. Academic Positioning & Scope Boundaries

### What DecisionSphere Is NOT Claiming (Anti-Novelty Declaration)
1. We did NOT invent XGBoost, Random Forests, Ridge Regression, or TreeSHAP.
2. We did NOT discover that promotions increase sales, that tight lead times increase late delivery risk, or that overtime increases employee attrition.
3. We do NOT claim that Rossmann, DataCo, and IBM HR Analytics represent observations from a single real-world company.
4. We do NOT claim that our predictive scenario simulations constitute proved causal interventions (adhering strictly to Shmueli 2010).
5. We do NOT claim that recommendations are mathematically optimal unless an explicit objective function and constraint set are defined and solved.

### What DecisionSphere IS Claiming (Defensible Research Contribution)
> **Primary Scientific Contribution:**  
> A formal, auditable **Scenario-Driven Cross-Functional Decision Intelligence Framework** that:
> 1. Formally couples heterogeneous predictive machine learning models across demand, supply chain, and workforce domains through explicit parametric shock propagation.
> 2. Enforces a strict 6-tier epistemic separation between empirical observation, statistical prediction, scenario assumption, propagation modeling, deterministic financial accounting, and rule-based recommendation.
> 3. Discovers and quantifies cross-functional trade-offs (e.g., hidden losses from logistics penalties and attrition replacement costs) that single-domain predictive optimizations systematically fail to surface.
> 4. Evaluates the stability and defensibility of scenario-based recommendations through explainable feature attribution (TreeSHAP) and parametric sensitivity analysis of cross-domain coupling assumptions.
