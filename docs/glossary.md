## 1. Introduction & Epistemic Framework

This glossary establishes the formal nomenclature, mathematical definitions, and technical interpretations for all variables, metrics, models, algorithms, and governance gates within DecisionSphere.

To preserve scientific rigor, every concept in DecisionSphere is contextualized across the **6-Tier Epistemic Hierarchy** (Section 2) and adheres to the **Anti-Causality Rule**:
$$\text{Conditional Prediction } P(Y \mid X) \neq \text{Causal Effect } \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \text{do}(X)]$$
$$\text{SHAP Attribution } \phi_i(f, x) \neq \text{Direct Causal Mechanism}$$

---

## 2. The 6-Tier Epistemic Hierarchy

| Tier | Name | Definition & Epistemic Boundary | Examples in DecisionSphere |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **Tier 1** | `OBSERVED` | Measured, historical ground truth directly extracted from benchmark datasets without modeling or synthetic transformation. | Historical Rossmann daily sales, DataCo order shipping modes, IBM HR baseline employee counts. |
| **Tier 2** | `PREDICTED` | Statistical conditional inferences generated by offline-trained machine learning models ($f(X) \approx \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$ or $\hat{P}(Y=1 \mid X)$). | Forecasted Store 1 sales, Calibrated Late Delivery Probability ($p_{\text{late}}$), Calibrated Attrition Probability ($p_{\text{att}}$). |
| **Tier 3** | `ASSUMED` | Explicit operational constants and scenario levers specified by the user or organizational governance policy. | Demand growth $+15\%$, scheduled lead time offset $+1$ day, replacement cost per employee (\$30,000, `ASM-004`). |
| **Tier 4** | `PROPAGATED` | Cross-domain transformations translated via algorithmic coupling rules across operational boundaries. | Dispatch order scaling ($\Delta O$), operational workload pressure ($P_{\text{ops}}$), workforce overtime reclassification ($W_{\text{ot}}$). |
| **Tier 5** | `CALCULATED` | Deterministic mathematical accounting formulas linking operational outcomes to financial and composite risk metrics. | Gross Realized Revenue, COGS, Delivery Delay Penalty, Turnover Replacement Drag, Net Profit Delta, Composite Risk Score (CRS). |
| **Tier 6** | `RECOMMENDED` | Rule-grounded strategic actions synthesized by the policy engine based on computed risk thresholds and financial outcomes. | `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS`, `REJECT_RESTRUCTURE`, `HALT_FURTHER_INVESTIGATION`. |

## 3. Alphabetical Scientific & Technical Glossary

### ASM Parameters (Architectural Assumption Levers)
- **Simple Explanation:** A set of explicitly numbered constants that link operational metrics across organizational departments.
- **Technical Explanation:** Coupling constants (`ASM-001` through `ASM-008`) establishing the deterministic mapping between retail demand, logistics volume, and workforce capacity:
  - `ASM-001`: Selling price per demand unit ($P_{\text{unit}} = \$25.00$).
  - `ASM-002`: Cost of Goods Sold ratio ($r_{\text{COGS}} = 0.60$).
  - `ASM-003`: Base fulfillment logistics cost per order ($C_{\text{base}} = \$8.50$).
  - `ASM-004`: Turnover replacement cost per departed employee ($C_{\text{replace}} = \$30,000.00$).
  - `ASM-005`: Carrier late delivery penalty per late shipment ($C_{\text{penalty}} = \$25.00$).
  - `ASM-006`: WorkLifeBalance decrement induced by sustained overtime ($\Delta \text{WLB} = -1.0$).
  - `ASM-007`: Normalized enterprise baseline volume ($V_{\text{cycle}} = 10,000$ units).
  - `ASM-008`: Normalized logistics order batch scale ($O_{\text{baseline}} = 1,000$ shipments).
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Defined in `src/business/engine.py` and `src/scenario/engine.py`. Subject to automated sensitivity stress-testing.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/09_cross_domain_decision_pipeline.ipynb`, `notebooks/10_financial_accounting.ipynb`.

### Baseline
- **Simple Explanation:** The expected operational state before any scenario shocks or executive decisions are applied.
- **Technical Explanation:** The unperturbed reference vector $X_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ representing historical operating conditions over a standardized 14-day planning cycle, against which all scenario deltas ($\Delta Y = Y_{\text{scenario}} - Y_{\text{baseline}}$) are evaluated.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Evaluated dynamically during each simulation pass in `ScenarioEngine.run_scenario()`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/00_project_orientation.ipynb`, `notebooks/13_end_to_end_decision_walkthrough.ipynb`.

---

### Brier Score
- **Simple Explanation:** A mathematical score measuring how accurate and well-calibrated a probability model is.
- **Technical Explanation:** The mean squared error between predicted probabilities $\hat{p}_i \in [0, 1]$ and actual binary outcomes $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$:
  $$\text{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{p}_i - y_i)^2$$
  Decomposes mathematically into $\text{Reliability} - \text{Resolution} + \text{Uncertainty}$. Lower scores indicate superior probabilistic reliability.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Supply Chain model achieves $\text{BS} = 0.0886$ (calibrated); Workforce model achieves $\text{BS} = 0.0988$ (calibrated).
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/08_model_evaluation_and_comparison.ipynb`.

---

### Calibration (Probability Calibration)
- **Simple Explanation:** Ensuring that when a model predicts a $70\%$ probability of late delivery, roughly 70 out of 100 such orders actually arrive late.
- **Technical Explanation:** The statistical alignment between posterior score outputs $s(x) \in [0, 1]$ and true empirical posterior frequencies $P(Y=1 \mid s(x))$. Raw tree ensembles output margin scores that are distorted by non-probabilistic splitting criteria. DecisionSphere uses post-hoc **Sigmoid (Platt) Scaling**:
  $$\hat{P}(Y=1 \mid s) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(A \cdot s + B)}$$
  fitted via maximum likelihood on a disjoint temporal validation split.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Encapsulated within `CalibratedClassifierCV` inside `models/supply_chain/` and `models/workforce/`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`.

---

### Composite Risk Score (CRS)
- **Simple Explanation:** A single, normalized risk index from 0 to 100 that summarizes operational danger across sales, delivery, and workforce retention.
- **Technical Explanation:** A weighted Minkowski-style or linear aggregation of domain-specific normalized risk scores $R_d \in [0, 100]$:
  $$\text{CRS} = w_{\text{demand}} R_{\text{demand}} + w_{\text{supply}} R_{\text{supply}} + w_{\text{workforce}} R_{\text{workforce}}$$
  Default governance weights are balanced: $w = (0.333, 0.333, 0.334)$. Classified into discrete tiers:
  - Low Risk: $0 \le \text{CRS} < 30$
  - Medium Risk: $30 \le \text{CRS} < 60$
  - High Risk: $60 \le \text{CRS} < 80$
  - Critical Risk: $80 \le \text{CRS} \le 100$
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Implemented in `src/recommendation/engine.py` to drive high-level decision gating and policy warnings.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/11_risk_and_recommendation_engine.ipynb`.

---

### Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
- **Simple Explanation:** The direct inventory and manufacturing cost required to produce sold goods.
- **Technical Explanation:** Deterministic accounting cost scaled to realized demand volume:
  $$\text{COGS} = V_{\text{realized}} \times P_{\text{unit}} \times r_{\text{COGS}}$$
  where $r_{\text{COGS}} = 0.60$ under `ASM-002`.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Evaluated in `BusinessImpactEngine._compute_cogs()`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/10_financial_accounting.ipynb`.

---

### Cross-Domain Propagation
- **Simple Explanation:** The ripple effect where changes in commercial sales automatically alter warehouse workload, carrier delays, and staff overtime.
- **Technical Explanation:** Algorithmic translation pipeline executing deterministic dependency transformations:
  $$\Delta \text{Sales} \xrightarrow{\text{ASM-007}} \Delta \text{Dispatch Orders} \xrightarrow{f_{\text{supply}}} \hat{P}(\text{Late}) \xrightarrow{\text{ASM-008}} \text{Workload Pressure} \xrightarrow{\text{ASM-006}} \text{OverTime Shift} \xrightarrow{f_{\text{workforce}}} \hat{P}(\text{Attrition})$$
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Managed by `ScenarioEngine._propagate_shocks()`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/09_cross_domain_decision_pipeline.ipynb`.

---

### Decision Intelligence (DI)
- **Simple Explanation:** An engineering discipline that connects data analysis, machine learning forecasts, and business rules into actionable, traceable business recommendations.
- **Technical Explanation:** An applied scientific framework (Kozyrkov 2019, Pratt 2020) that augments operational decision-making by embedding statistical predictive models ($\mathcal{M}$) within deterministic optimization and accounting constraint systems ($\mathcal{F}$), generating traceable evidence graphs rather than ungrounded conversational text.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** The overarching architectural paradigm governing DecisionSphere.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/00_project_orientation.ipynb`.

---

### Gate G, Gate H, Gate I (Model Governance Gates)
- **Simple Explanation:** Rigorous validation checks that ensure model safety, data integrity, and recommendation policy compliance.
- **Technical Explanation:** Architectural enforcement gates implemented in `src/scenario/engine.py` and `src/recommendation/engine.py`:
  - **Gate G (Probabilistic Calibration Gate):** Verifies that predicted classification probabilities conform to isotonic/sigmoid calibration constraints with Brier score $\le 0.15$.
  - **Gate H (Model Hash & Artifact Integrity Gate):** Computes SHA-256 digests of all serialized model files (`demand_model.joblib`, etc.) and compares them against immutable cryptographic signatures to prevent silent model drift.
  - **Gate I (Policy Governance Interlock):** Prevents automated approval of any scenario where $\text{CRS} \ge 60$ or $\Delta \text{Net Profit} < 0$, mandating executive review or rejection.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Validated during startup, simulation execution, and recommendation generation.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/00_project_orientation.ipynb`, `notebooks/11_risk_and_recommendation_engine.ipynb`.

### Late Delivery Probability ($p_{\text{late}}$)
- **Simple Explanation:** The likelihood that a dispatched order will fail to arrive within its promised delivery window.
- **Technical Explanation:** The calibrated posterior probability $\hat{P}(Y=1 \mid X_{\text{order}})$ generated by the DataCo XGBoost classifier using legitimate order-time features (Scheduled Days SLA, Shipping Mode, Order Item Quantity, Customer Market).
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Produced by `SupplyChainClassifier.predict_proba()`. Drives fulfillment loss and carrier penalty accounting.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/10_financial_accounting.ipynb`.

### Lever (Scenario Lever)
- **Simple Explanation:** A business parameter or operational knob that an executive can adjust to test different strategies.
- **Technical Explanation:** An input parameter in the `ScenarioLevers` schema representing controllable organizational interventions:
  - Commercial Levers: `demand_growth_pct`, `promo_active`.
  - Logistics Levers: `scheduled_days_offset`, `shipping_mode_override`.
  - Workforce Levers: `overtime_workforce_pct`, `salary_hike_override`.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Defined in `src/scenario/schema.py`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/00_project_orientation.ipynb`, `notebooks/13_end_to_end_decision_walkthrough.ipynb`.

### Overtime Cost
- **Simple Explanation:** Extra payroll expenses incurred when employees work beyond standard working hours.
- **Technical Explanation:** Incremental labor cost calculated based on baseline payroll, the proportion of employees on overtime, and premium hourly rates:
  $$\Delta C_{\text{ot}} = W_{\text{headcount}} \times r_{\text{ot\_headcount}} \times \text{Salary}_{\text{monthly}} \times 0.25$$
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Evaluated in `BusinessImpactEngine._compute_workforce_costs()`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/10_financial_accounting.ipynb`.

---

### PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under Curve)
- **Simple Explanation:** An accuracy metric that measures model effectiveness specifically on rare events like employee turnover.
- **Technical Explanation:** The integral of the precision-recall curve $\int_0^1 P(R) dR$. Unlike ROC-AUC, which can present overly optimistic figures on heavily imbalanced datasets ($16.1\%$ positive class in IBM HR), PR-AUC evaluates true positive identification against false positive burdens without being inflated by true negative counts.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** IBM HR XGBoost classifier achieves $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.5284$ against a naive no-skill baseline of $0.1610$.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/08_model_evaluation_and_comparison.ipynb`.

---

### Provenance (Data & Model Lineage)
- **Simple Explanation:** A complete digital audit trail showing exactly which data, model versions, and settings generated a result.
- **Technical Explanation:** Cryptographic audit trail recording scenario parameters, execution timestamps, source dataset versions, model SHA-256 digests, and calculation versions in an immutable metadata envelope attached to every simulation response.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Returned in FastAPI simulation payloads and rendered in the Next.js Audit / Provenance views.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/01_data_inventory_and_provenance.ipynb`.

---

### ROC-AUC (Receiver Operating Characteristic Area Under Curve)
- **Simple Explanation:** A metric evaluating how well a classifier separates positive cases from negative cases across all thresholds.
- **Technical Explanation:** The area under the curve plotting True Positive Rate vs False Positive Rate across all discrimination thresholds $\tau \in [0, 1]$. Measures ranking capability independent of threshold selection.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Supply Chain model achieves $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.7710$; Workforce model achieves $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.8143$.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`.

---

### Shock (Scenario Shock)
- **Simple Explanation:** A sudden internal or external change applied to the business system to test its resilience.
- **Technical Explanation:** An exogenous perturbation applied to baseline variables (e.g., $+30\%$ retail demand shock, $-2$ days shipping lead-time reduction) designed to stress-test capacity limits and risk propagation.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Encapsulated within `ScenarioLevers`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/00_project_orientation.ipynb`.

---

### TreeSHAP (Tree Shapley Additive Explanations)
- **Simple Explanation:** A mathematical technique that shows exactly how much each feature contributed to a specific model prediction.
- **Technical Explanation:** An efficient polynomial-time algorithm ($O(TLD^2)$) computing exact game-theoretic Shapley values for tree-based ensemble models (Lundberg et al. 2020):
  $$f(x) = \phi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^M \phi_i(f, x)$$
  where $\phi_i$ represents the localized contribution of feature $i$ relative to expected model base output $\phi_0$.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Utilized in `notebooks/05_demand_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, and `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/05_demand_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`.

---

### Turnover Replacement Cost
- **Simple Explanation:** The substantial expense required to recruit, hire, and onboard a replacement worker when an employee leaves.
- **Technical Explanation:** The organizational friction cost evaluated under `ASM-004`:
  $$\Delta C_{\text{turnover}} = W_{\text{headcount}} \times \Delta p_{\text{att}} \times C_{\text{replace}}$$
  where $C_{\text{replace}} = \$30,000.00$.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Evaluated in `BusinessImpactEngine._compute_workforce_costs()`. Represents the primary mechanism causing negative net profit during promotional surges.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/10_financial_accounting.ipynb`.

---

### WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error)
- **Simple Explanation:** The primary accuracy metric for sales forecasting that penalizes big errors on high-volume days more than small errors on quiet days.
- **Technical Explanation:** A scale-independent time-series error metric:
  $$\text{WAPE} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T |y_t - \hat{y}_t|}{\sum_{t=1}^T y_t}$$
  Avoids division-by-zero anomalies and distortions characteristic of standard MAPE when sales are zero or near-zero.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** Demand forecasting XGBoost model achieves holdout $\text{WAPE} = 11.23\%$.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/05_demand_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/08_model_evaluation_and_comparison.ipynb`.

---

### XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting)
- **Simple Explanation:** A high-performance machine learning algorithm that builds a sequence of decision trees, where each tree fixes the errors made by previous trees.
- **Technical Explanation:** A scalable gradient tree boosting framework (Chen & Guestrin 2016) minimizing a regularized objective:
  $$\mathcal{L}^{(t)} = \sum_{i=1}^n l\left(y_i, \hat{y}_i^{(t-1)} + f_t(x_i)\right) + \Omega(f_t)$$
  using second-order Taylor expansion and exact greedy/approximate split finding algorithms.
- **DecisionSphere Usage:** The standard predictive engine for all three domains in `src/models/`.
- **Notebook Reference:** `notebooks/05_demand_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/06_supply_chain_model.ipynb`, `notebooks/07_workforce_model.ipynb`.
